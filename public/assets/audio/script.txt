YASAWI — Mausoleum of Khoja Ahmed Yasawi
Mausoleum of Khoja Ahmed Yasawi. The UNESCO jewel of southern Kazakhstan. Begun in 1389 on the orders of Amir Temur, this vast brick mausoleum honours the 12th-century Sufi poet and teacher Khoja Ahmed Yasawi. Its turquoise dome, one of the largest in Central Asia, rises above a monumental portal covered in glazed tile. The building was never finished, and that is part of its charm: look at the unplastered brick of the back walls to see how Timurid builders worked. It is still an active pilgrimage site for Muslims across Central Asia. A tip for your visit: Dress modestly: shoulders and knees covered; women may need a headscarf inside.

ESIM — Esim Khan Mausoleum
Esim Khan Mausoleum. A khan of the Kazakhs rests beside the saint. Esim Khan ruled the Kazakh Khanate in the early 17th century and made Turkestan his capital. His small, austere mausoleum stands inside the Yasawi complex, a few steps from the great portal. Several khans and nobles were buried here over the centuries: being laid to rest next to Khoja Ahmed Yasawi was the highest honour of the steppe. A tip for your visit: Look for the simple brick form: it was built for a warrior, not for show.

HILVET — Hilvet Underground Mosque
Hilvet Underground Mosque. The saint’s underground retreat. Beside the mausoleum lies the small underground chamber where Khoja Ahmed Yasawi is said to have spent his final years in meditation. Narrow steps lead down to a cool, quiet cell. It is a tiny space, so visits are short and respectful. A tip for your visit: Remove shoes where indicated.

RABIGA — Rabiga Sultan Begim Mausoleum
Rabiga Sultan Begim Mausoleum. A 15th-century royal tomb with a sky-blue dome. Close to the Yasawi complex stands the mausoleum of Rabiga Sultan Begim, a granddaughter of the astronomer-king Ulugh Beg. The compact building is topped with a ribbed blue dome and is surrounded by quiet gardens. It is a peaceful stop that is rarely crowded. A tip for your visit: Combine with the Yasawi mausoleum in one walk.

MOSQUE — Khoja Ahmed Yasawi Mosque
Khoja Ahmed Yasawi Mosque. The new grand mosque beside the ancient complex. Just south of the mausoleum rises Turkestan’s new grand mosque, opened in 2026 and built as a gift from Uzbekistan to the people of Kazakhstan. Four minarets, a vast prayer hall for more than 5,000 worshippers and traditional aivans echo the Timurid forms of the old complex in modern materials. Fountains, gardens and evening lighting around it have turned the southern edge of the historic centre into a new promenade. A tip for your visit: Active mosque: modest dress, shoes off inside, no visits during prayer.

JUMA — Juma Mosque of Turkestan
Juma Mosque of Turkestan. A wooden Friday mosque from 1878. The Juma (Friday) Mosque was built in 1878 with carved wooden columns and a flat beamed ceiling in the Central Asian tradition. It is the oldest working mosque in the city and a quiet counterpoint to the monumental mausoleum next door. Inside, light falls between slender painted pillars; outside, the courtyard fills with worshippers on Friday afternoons. A tip for your visit: Ask permission before photographing inside.

KULTOBE — Kultobe Ancient Settlement
Kultobe Ancient Settlement. Dig sites beneath the old citadel mound. Kultobe is the hill that preserves the layers of ancient Yasy, the city that grew into Turkestan. Archaeologists have uncovered streets, workshops and kilns here dating back more than a thousand years. Climb the mound at sunset for a view of the mausoleum’s dome over the modern city. A tip for your visit: Wear sturdy shoes; paths are uneven.

HAMAM — Historic Bath House (Hammam)
Historic Bath House (Hammam). A restored 16th-century public bath. Close to the mausoleum, this historic bath house shows how travellers on the Silk Road washed and rested. Its brick domes and heating system are well preserved and form a small museum. Look for the carved niches and the clever underfloor heating channels. A tip for your visit: Bring a camera for the domed ceilings.

MUSEUM — Azret Sultan Museum-Reserve
Azret Sultan Museum-Reserve. The story of Turkestan in one place. The museum-reserve looks after the Yasawi complex and the old town. Its halls explain the history of Turkestan as a spiritual and political capital of the Kazakh Khanate. Exhibits include manuscripts, ceramics, jewellery and models of the historic city. A tip for your visit: Ask about English audio guides.

KARAVAN — Karavan Saray
Karavan Saray. A caravanserai-style quarter of shops and workshops. Karavan Saray recreates the atmosphere of a Silk Road inn, with arched courtyards, craft workshops, souvenir shops and cafes. Watch artisans make felt, leather and jewellery, and buy a handmade gift directly from the maker. A tip for your visit: Prices are fair but you may politely ask for a better price.

AMPH — Turkestan Amphitheatre
Turkestan Amphitheatre. Open-air shows on the Silk Road stage. The amphitheatre next to the Karavan Saray hosts concerts, folklore shows and festivals under the open sky, with the mausoleum’s dome on the horizon. In summer evenings this is where the city gathers: music, lights on the water channels and food stalls along the promenade. A tip for your visit: Check the programme at the Karavan Saray ticket office.

FOUNTAIN — Musical Fountain
Musical Fountain. A lotus of water and light on summer nights. The singing fountain next to the amphitheatre is shaped like a lotus and surrounded by ten smaller jets. In the evening its columns of water, up to 15 metres high, dance to music and coloured light. It was a gift from East Kazakhstan for the city’s rebirth in 2019 and is now the favourite evening meeting point of local families. A tip for your visit: Shows run from late spring to early autumn.

ARBAT — Turkestan Arbat
Turkestan Arbat. The city’s pedestrian promenade. Turkestan’s Arbat is a pedestrian street with a stage, lighting installations and a 150-metre illuminated truss above the walkway. Street musicians, ice cream, souvenirs and free Wi-Fi make it the easiest place to feel the rhythm of the new city. It links the historic centre with the Karavan Saray complex, so it is a natural evening stroll between the two. A tip for your visit: Try the local ice cream and fresh juice stands.

THEATRE — Turkestan Music and Drama Theatre
Turkestan Music and Drama Theatre. 510 seats of Kazakh and world drama. Opened in 2021, the theatre blends classical and oriental styles and seats 510. Its first production was “Borte”, about the wife of Genghis Khan; today the repertoire ranges from Kazakh epics to musicals and concerts. Even without a ticket, the façade and the square in front are worth a look on the way along the Arbat. A tip for your visit: Buy tickets at the box office or online a few days ahead.

TAIKAZAN — Taikazan Monument
Taikazan Monument. A two-tonne bronze echo of the sacred cauldron. The original Taikazan, a bronze cauldron of two tonnes cast in 1399 for the Yasawi mausoleum, is a symbol of hospitality and the unity of the Turkic peoples. A full-size bronze replica stands in a small square on Nursultan Nazarbayev Street, where you can walk around it and touch it. The original holds pride of place in the Kazandyk hall of the mausoleum; see both and compare. A tip for your visit: Locals make a wish with a hand on the rim.

GAUHAR — Gauhar Ana Mausoleum
Gauhar Ana Mausoleum. The resting place of Yasawi’s daughter. Gauhar Ana was the daughter of Khoja Ahmed Yasawi. Her mausoleum stands on the southern edge of the city and is a popular pilgrimage stop for women asking for family blessings. The present building is modest, but the site has been venerated for centuries and the cemetery around it is one of the oldest in Turkestan. A tip for your visit: A headscarf is expected for women.

YURT — Kazakh Yurt Experience
Kazakh Yurt Experience. Tea, felt and storytelling in a traditional yurt. Just outside town you can visit a family-run yurt camp. Hosts show how the felt-lined tent is raised, serve hot tea with baursaks, and play the dombra, the two-string Kazakh lute. Overnight stays are possible in spring and autumn. A tip for your visit: Locations vary; arrange through your hotel or a local host.

BAZAAR — Turkestan Central Bazaar
Turkestan Central Bazaar. Spices, dried fruit, bread and fresh dairy. Southern Kazakhstan’s bazaars are the best place to taste the region. Pyramids of dried apricots and raisins sit beside rounds of fresh bread, sheep-milk cheese (kurt) and jars of honey. Ask to taste before buying and try shubat, a tangy fermented camel milk. A tip for your visit: Carry small bills.

KITCHEN — Kazakh Cuisine Quarter
Kazakh Cuisine Quarter. Beshbarmak, baursak and tea. Sample the national dish beshbarmak, tender boiled meat served over broad noodles in a rich broth. Round it off with warm baursaks (fried dough) and endless pots of tea. Many restaurants also serve Uzbek-style plov and samsa, a result of the region’s shared history. A tip for your visit: Dishes are shared; order less than you think.

JIBEK — Jibek Joly Amusement Park
Jibek Joly Amusement Park. Rides, a Ferris wheel and green alleys. Jibek Joly (“Silk Road”) park in the new part of the city has fourteen Italian-built rides, a Ferris wheel with a view over the steppe, shaded alleys, playgrounds and sports courts. It is the place to take children after a morning of mausoleums, and a good spot to watch the sunset over the flat horizon. A tip for your visit: Rides open from late afternoon in summer heat.

ARENA — Turkestan Arena
Turkestan Arena. A 7,000-seat stadium and the home of FC Turan. Turkestan Arena is the city’s modern stadium, home of FC Turan and the venue of the 2021 Kazakhstan Super Cup. Its sweeping roof is one of the landmarks of the new administrative district. A football evening here is an easy way to meet locals: tickets are cheap and the atmosphere is friendly. A tip for your visit: Check the Kazakhstan Premier League schedule.

TULIPS — Karatau Foothills & Spring Tulips
Karatau Foothills & Spring Tulips. A red carpet of wild tulips in April. From early April to mid May the foothills of the Karatau range near Kentau turn red and yellow with wild tulips and poppies. It is a short season and a magical time to visit. Hire a local driver to find the best fields, and combine the trip with a picnic. A tip for your visit: Look, do not pick: wild tulips are protected.

AKSU — Aksu-Zhabagly Nature Reserve
Aksu-Zhabagly Nature Reserve. Kazakhstan’s oldest reserve in the Western Tian Shan. Founded in 1926, Aksu-Zhabagly protects alpine meadows, canyons and glaciers, along with snow leopards, ibex and rare tulip species. The reserve is part of a UNESCO World Heritage site. Hiking trails, waterfalls and the Aksu canyon are the highlights. A tip for your visit: A permit is required; arrange through the reserve office.

ARYSTANBAB — Arystan Bab Mausoleum
Arystan Bab Mausoleum. Resting place of Yasawi’s teacher. Arystan Bab was the spiritual teacher of Khoja Ahmed Yasawi. His mausoleum, rebuilt in the 14th century, is a pilgrimage site set in a rural village south of Turkestan. Most visitors combine it with a trip to the nearby steppe. A tip for your visit: Modest dress is expected.

SAURAN — Sauran Ancient City
Sauran Ancient City. A fortified medieval trade centre. Sauran was a thriving Silk Road city with massive earth walls, a citadel and a complex water system. Today you can walk along the huge ramparts and imagine the caravans. It is a haunting, empty landscape, ideal for photographers. A tip for your visit: There are few facilities; carry water.

OTRAR — Otrar Ancient City
Otrar Ancient City. Birthplace of the philosopher Al-Farabi. Otrar was a major Silk Road crossroads and the home of the polymath Al-Farabi. In 1219 the city fell to the Mongols, an event that changed Central Asian history. Excavations and a museum bring the ruins to life. A tip for your visit: Roads can be rough after rain.

SAYRAM — Sayram & Ibrahim-Ata Shrine
Sayram & Ibrahim-Ata Shrine. One of the oldest towns in Central Asia. Sayram has existed for more than two thousand years. Its old quarter and the shrine of Ibrahim-Ata, father of Khoja Ahmed Yasawi, are important for pilgrims. It sits near Shymkent, so it works well as a day trip. A tip for your visit: Ask a guide to explain the shrines.

DOMALAK — Domalak Ana Mausoleum
Domalak Ana Mausoleum. The mother of the Great Horde, honoured in the hills. Domalak Ana, the wise wife of the ancestor Baidibek, is revered as the mother of three great Kazakh tribes. Her white marble mausoleum rises on a hill in the Karatau foothills, rebuilt in 1996 on the site of the medieval tomb. Pilgrims come year-round; the views over the steppe and the quiet of the place make it a worthwhile day trip. A tip for your visit: Go with a driver; there is no public transport.
